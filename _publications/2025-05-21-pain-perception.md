A complex interplay of psychological, biological, and social factors influences pain perception and response patterns. This study aims to identify the determinants of maladaptive pain perception and response patterns through predictive analysis using psychological variables such as resilience, neuroticism, extraversion, grit, and optimism. A quantitative approach was used, incorporating predictive modeling techniques (decision trees, XGBoost, and Random Forests) to assess pain catastrophizing and sensitivity among 305 Indians. The XGBoost classifier predicting clinically significant pain catastrophizing achieved an accuracy of 74%. Findings indicate that neuroticism is a key predictor of pain-related outcomes, with resilience and optimism serving as protective factors. The study highlights the potential for personalized interventions by utilizing machine learning models to optimize predictor levels for improved pain management. The results underscore the need for further research incorporating biological and environmental factors to develop holistic pain management strategies.
